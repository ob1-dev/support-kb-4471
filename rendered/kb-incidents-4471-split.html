#4471 is not one issue: what the merged reports show

owner Chris O'Brien open reviewed 2026-10-28 · 0 days ago

#4471 is marked known: one issue, one fix queued. The reports merged into it say otherwise. They were merged on subject line alone (decision 0001), and “wrong day on the invite” is what every date bug looks like from the outside. Read by cause, the reports hold separate problems, and only one of them is the parser fix that is queued.

The queued parser fix will not close #4471. Each problem below has its own ticket, with how many reports it covers.

parser: day and month swapped by the shared parser

The shared parser reads every date month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first, so a booking on D/M lands on M/D. In every one of these reports that names both dates, the second is the first with day and month swapped. The swapped day is always 12 or lower, so the wrong date is still a valid date and nothing errors.

hint: the booking-v3 form tells people to type month first

booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field and then reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. People type what the hint says. The swap happens in the form, so the parser fix does not touch it. The #4471 page already flags this row as “not this ticket”, but it had no ticket of its own.

one-day-move: a day off, not a swap

The desk noted these as one-day moves (desk-notes.json) and files them under parser by tenant, where they are counted. They are still a different problem, so they have their own ticket. Tuesday 12 May shows as Monday 11 May; a client in Lisbon gets Tuesday and one in Toronto gets Monday for the same meeting; an invite arrives before the booking. That points at time-zone handling, not date order. One reporter says they have stopped reporting these, so the real number is likely higher.

What to change on the desk

  • Do not close #4471 on the parser fix. Close each ticket on its own fix.
  • When intake tag and tenant disagree, trust the tenant: the cause depends on the tenant’s locale and form.
  • Matching on subject merges different bugs that look the same to the reporter. Before marking a big ticket known, split it by cause.
